# (08a) Fubini’s Theorem <!-- % 푸비니 정리 -->

Let $f : X \times Y \to \mathbb{C}$ and write  
\[
f_x(y) = f(x,y), \qquad f^y(x) = f(x,y).
\]

## Tonelli’s Theorem <!-- % 토넬리 정리 -->

If $f : (\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{T})$ - measurable and $f \ge 0$, then

1. For each $x$,  
   $f_x : Y \to \lbrack 0,\infty\rbrack$ is $\mathcal{T}$-measurable.

2. For each $y$,  
   $f^y : X \to \lbrack 0,\infty\rbrack$ is $\mathcal{S}$-measurable.

3. The functions  
\[
x \mapsto \int_Y f_x\, d\nu
\quad\text{and}\quad  
y \mapsto \int_X f^y\, d\mu
\]
are $\mathcal{S}$-measurable and $\mathcal{T}$-measurable.

Moreover,
\[
\int_{X\times Y} f\, d(\mu\times\nu)
=
\int_X \left( \int_Y f_x \, d\nu \right) d\mu
=
\int_Y \left( \int_X f^y \, d\mu \right) d\nu.
\]

---

## Fubini’s Theorem <!-- % 푸비니 정리 -->

If $f \in L^1(\mu\times\nu)$, i.e.
\[
\int_{X\times Y} \lvert f(x,y)\rvert \, d(\mu\times\nu) < \infty,
\]
then:

- $f_x \in L^1(\nu)$ for a.e. $x$  
- $f^y \in L^1(\mu)$ for a.e. $y$

Define
\[
\varphi(x) = \int_Y f_x\, d\nu(y),
\qquad
\psi(y) = \int_X f^y\, d\mu(x).
\]

Then
\[
\varphi \in L^1(\mu), \qquad \psi \in L^1(\nu),
\]
and
\[
\int_{X\times Y} f\, d(\mu\times\nu)
=
\int_X \varphi\, d\mu
=
\int_Y \psi \, d\nu.
\]

# (08b) Borel Product & Lebesgue Measure <!-- % 보렐 측도와 르벡측도 -->

Consider $(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}, \mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L}, m\times m)$.

Let  
\[
\mathcal{L}_2 = (\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L})^\ast
\]
be the **completion** of the product measure.

## Definition: Completion of a measure space <!-- % 완비화 정의 -->
For a measure space $(X, \mathcal{A}, \mu)$, the *completion* is the process of
enlarging $\mathcal{A}$ by including all subsets of measure–zero sets.
Formally, the completed $\sigma$–algebra is  
\[
\mathcal{A}^\ast
=
\lbrace E \subseteq X :
\exists A,B \in \mathcal{A},\ A \subseteq E \subseteq B,\ 
\mu(B \setminus A)=0 \rbrace.
\]
This makes every null set and every subset of a null set measurable.  


---

We conclude
\[
\mathcal{B}_2 \subseteq \mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L} \subseteq (\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L})^\ast.
\]

### pf)
#### 1. $\mathcal{B}_2 \subseteq \mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L}$

- $\mathcal{B}_2$ is the **Borel σ-algebra** on $\mathbb{R}^2$, generated by all open sets.  
- Every open set in $\mathbb{R}^2$ belongs to $\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L}$ because:
  - open sets in $\mathbb{R}$ are Lebesgue measurable,
  - products of Lebesgue measurable sets are measurable in $\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L}$,
  - $\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L}$ is a σ-algebra and contains all measurable rectangles.
- Since $\mathcal{B}_2$ is the *smallest* σ-algebra containing all open sets,  
  it must be contained in **any** σ-algebra that contains all open sets.

#### 2. $\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L} \subseteq (\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L})^\ast$

- $(\mathcal{L}\times\mathcal{L})^\ast$ is the **completion** of the product σ-algebra.  
- By definition of completion,
  \[
  \mathcal{A} \subseteq \mathcal{A}^\ast
  \qquad\text{for any σ-algebra } \mathcal{A}.
  \]
- Thus every set in the Lebesgue product σ-algebra automatically belongs to its completion.

---

## Theorem 8-3.

If $f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{C}$ is $\mathcal{L}$-measurable, then  

> $\exists\; g: \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{C}$ Borel–measurable with $f=g\;$ a.e.

### pf) 

Let $f = \sum_{k=1}^\infty c_k \cdot \chi_{E_k}$.

Take $g = \sum_{k=1}^\infty c_k \cdot \chi_{A_k}$ s.t.
- $A_k, B_k$ Borel-measurable,  
- $A_k \subseteq E \subseteq B_k$,
- $(m \times m)^\ast(B_k\setminus A_k)=0$.


## Property 8-1.

If $f : \mathbb{R}^2 \to \mathbb{C}$ is $\mathcal{L}$-measurable, then 

\[
\Rightarrow f = g + h
\]
, wherer $g$ is Borel-measurable and $h$ is zero a.e.

# (08c) Convolution of Integrable Functions <!-- % 적분가능 함수의 곱하기(합성곱) -->

Let $f,g \in L^1(\mathbb{R})$.

## Definition: Convolution

For $f, g \in L^1(\mathbb{R})$,
\[
(f\ast g)(x)
=
\int_{\mathbb{R}} f(y)\, g(x-y)\, dy.
\]

This corresponds to the “real” version of the group algebra convolution.

---

## Basic Computation

Using Fubini’s Theorem and translation invariance of Lebesgue measure:

\[
\int_{\mathbb{R}} \left\lbrace \int_{\mathbb{R}} \lvert f(y)g(x-y) \rvert \, dx \right\rbrace dy
\le
\int_{\mathbb{R}} \left\lbrace \int_{\mathbb{R}} \lvert f(y)\rvert \; \lvert g(x-y) \rvert \, dx \right\rbrace dy
\]
\[
= 
\int_{\mathbb{R}} \left\lbrace \lvert f(y) \rvert \int_{\mathbb{R}} \lvert g(x-y) \rvert \, dx \right\rbrace dy
\]
\[
= \int_{\mathbb{R}} \left\lbrace \lvert f(y) \rvert \int_{\mathbb{R}} \lvert g(x) \rvert \, dx \right\rbrace dy
\]
\[
= \int_{\mathbb{R}} \lvert f(y) \rvert \, dy \cdot \int_{\mathbb{R}} \lvert g(x) \rvert \, dx
\]

Thus,
\[
\lVert f\ast g \rVert_1 \le \lVert f\rVert_1 \lVert g\rVert_1.
\]

This shows that $(L^1(\mathbb{R}), \ast)$ is a **Banach algebra**.

## Definition: Banach Algebra 

A **Banach algebra** is a mathematical structure that combines:

1. **Banach space**  
   A complete normed vector space (every Cauchy sequence converges).

2. **Algebra structure**  
   A multiplication operation $a \ast b$ defined on the space
   that is associative and bilinear.

3. **Norm compatibility**  
   The multiplication satisfies
   \[
   \lVert a \ast b \rVert \le \lVert a \rVert \, \lVert b \rVert.
   \]

---

## Algebraic Properties

For $f,g,h\in L^1$:

1. $(f\ast g)\ast h = f\ast(g\ast h)$  
2. $(af + bg)\ast h = af\ast h + bg\ast h$  
3. $f\ast g = g\ast f$  
4. $(f\ast g)' = f\ast g'$

---

## Approximation via Convolution

Let $1<p,q<\infty$ and $\frac{1}{p} + \frac{1}{q} = 1$.

If $f\in L^p(\mathbb{R})$ and $g\in L^q(\mathbb{R})$, then
\[
f\ast g \in L^\infty.
\]

### pf)  
Hölder’s inequality gives:
\[
\lvert(f\ast g)(x)\rvert
= \left\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}} f(y) g(x-y)\, dy\right\rvert
= \int_{\mathbb{R}} \lvert f(y) g_{-x}(-y) \rvert \, dy
\le \lVert f\rVert_p \lVert g\rVert_q.
\]

---

## Theorem 8-4.
If $f\in L^p(\mathbb{R})$ and $g\in L^q(\mathbb{R})$, then  
\[
f\ast g \in C_0(\mathbb{R}).
\]

### pf) 

Let $x_n\to x$. Then
\[
(f\ast g)(x_n) - (f\ast g)(x)
=
\int_{\mathbb{R}} f(y)g(x_n-y)\, dy
-
\int_{\mathbb{R}} f(y)g(x-y)\, dy.
\]

Rewrite:
\[
(f\ast g)(x_n)-(f\ast g)(x)
=
\int_{\mathbb{R}} f(y) \lbrack g(x_n-y)-g(x-y) \rbrack \, dy.
\]

Let $h_n := x_n - x$,
\[
(f \ast g)(x_n) - (f \ast g)(x)
=
\int_{\mathbb{R}} f(y)\, \lbrack g(x + h_n - y) - g(x - y) \rbrack \, dy
\]
\[
=
\int_{\mathbb{R}} f(x - t)\, \lbrack g(t + h_n) - g(t) \rbrack \, dt.
\]


Let $\tau_{h} g(t) = g(t + h)$ and using **Hölder's inequality**,
\[
\lvert (f \ast g)(x_n) - (f \ast g)(x) \rvert
\le
\lVert f \rVert_p \, \lVert \tau_{h_n} g - g \rVert_q.
\]

By the previously established result on the $L^{q}$–continuity of translations,
\[
h_n \to 0 \;\Rightarrow\; \lVert \tau_{h_n} g - g \rVert_q \to 0.
\]

Consequently,
\[
(f \ast g)(x_n) \to (f \ast g)(x),
\]
which shows that $f \ast g$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}$.
